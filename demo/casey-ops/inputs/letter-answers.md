## One time you made a process or vendor relationship work better: what was broken, what you did, what changed

There was no formal documentation around what steps and questions we'd be going through, what we were evaluating, and what problem we were trying to solve. We found that we were kind of explaining that every time we talked to a new vendor.

I produced a brief of that kind of stuff and then requested their feedback to directly answer those specific questions. After selecting a vendor, one of my things was to make sure that it was always a win-win scenario. I was trying to understand what was motivating them, and then they understood what was motivating us because, from a process perspective, if it didn't work out, if it wasn't a good relationship, then we'd have to find a new vendor. That would be wasting more time.

My goal was to do that, and ultimately it did save a lot of back-and-forth. Everybody knew it was very clear: clearly communicated what we were looking for, how things were being evaluated, and the process was a lot smoother. Just selecting a vendor became more systematic, smooth, and objective. It wasn't about who gave the best pitch. It was who solved the problem the best.

Ultimately, that saved the company a lot of time and a lot of money. It's hard to put an actual error rate and dollar amount because it was kind of just a general thing over time.
